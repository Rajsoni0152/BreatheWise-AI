Atmospheric Smog Science & Defense Guide

Clear, actionable education on winter temperature inversions, aerosol sizes, and respirator mechanics.

Why Does Smog Stagnate in Winter?

During clear, cold winter nights, the ground cools rapidly via thermal radiation. A layer of cold, dense air gets trapped close to the ground beneath a cap of warmer air aloft—a phenomenon called a Thermal Inversion.

This warm cap acts as an atmospheric lid. Tailpipe exhaust, crop smoke, and road dust cannot rise vertically and remain compressed at human breathing height until midday sunshine breaks the layer.

Why N95 Masks Work & Cloth Fails

Fine particulate matter (PM2.5) measures 2.5 microns or smaller (1/30th the diameter of human hair). Standard surgical and cloth masks have weave pores of 20 to 100 microns and leak heavily around the nose and cheeks.

Certified N95 / FFP2 respirators use electrostatically charged meltblown polypropylene fiber layers that trap particles down to 0.3 microns with at least 95% efficiency when properly molded to your nose bridge.
